# Rescue sequence playtest

Run a first round with 5–8 players on the same build. Do not explain the solutions. Let each person play the four lessons, then the four chain puzzles. Note where they freeze, whether they discover TURN and dragging placed tools, and whether they understand that redirected parcels remain dangerous. Give the first hint only after they request help; record that request.

For each chain puzzle, record time to first solution, attempts, tools/directions changed between attempts, hints used, and whether the failure marker explains the mistake. After the first win, offer five more minutes with the same puzzle and its optional goals. Watch whether they voluntarily try another plan, improve parcel safety, reduce tools, or watch their replay.

Ask: “What caused your last failure?”, “What did you expect that tool to do?”, and “What would you try next?” The key signal is experimentation after the player already knows a solution. An authored solution passing automated checks is only a prerequisite.

| Puzzle | Observation to resolve |
| --- | --- |
| Wrong Address | Does the parcel → switch → gate connection make sense? Can players find the shield-left + crate-on-switch alternative? |
| Fragile Delivery | Do players anticipate a second impact and add the catcher, rather than treating shielding as completion? |
| Air Mail | Are fan direction, reach, and the pressure plate understandable without a hint? |
| Express Route | Does the crate/launch/platform sequence feel intentional? Is the freeze window forgiving? |

Revise the least understood interaction before adding content. Repeat the round after changes. Add ropes or seesaws only after players find the existing combinations worth experimenting with. Separately verify the Android device checklist: installation/update signing, portrait cutouts, touch targets, Back, background/resume, sound/haptics, persistence and sustained frame rate.
